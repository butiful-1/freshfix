# What Can I Make with Salmon?

Salmon is one of those proteins that rewards a little attention without demanding much of it. A salmon fillet with olive oil, garlic, lemon, and salt roasted at 400°F for 12 minutes is genuinely one of the best things you can put on a weeknight dinner table — fast, flavorful, and impressive without any particular skill. That same fillet the next day goes into a grain bowl, a wrap, a pasta, or a salad without any additional cooking.

It's also one of the most nutritionally complete foods available. The omega-3 fatty acids, high-quality protein, vitamin D, and B vitamins make salmon one of the ingredients that actually delivers on the promise of food that's both good and good for you. And unlike a lot of health food, salmon tastes like something worth eating — rich, satisfying, and comfortable in a wide range of preparations from casual weeknight to dinner party centerpiece.

If you've got salmon and you're wondering what to make, here's everything it can do.

---

## Why Salmon Belongs in Every Kitchen

Salmon is rich in high-quality protein, an excellent source of omega-3 fatty acids, naturally loaded with vitamin D and B vitamins, quick to cook, and versatile enough to work baked, grilled, pan-seared, air-fried, or poached. Fresh salmon fillets cook in 12 to 15 minutes. Canned salmon requires no cooking at all and works in salmon cakes, salads, sandwiches, and quick lunches with almost no effort. Frozen salmon fillets thaw overnight in the refrigerator and perform as well as fresh in most cooked applications.

For anyone cooking more intentionally — whether that's managing portions, eating more whole foods, or supporting GLP-1 medication — salmon is one of the most valuable proteins available. The combination of high-quality protein and healthy fat creates genuine satiety, and the cooking time is short enough that it's realistic for a weeknight dinner rather than a weekend project.

## Breakfast

Salmon at breakfast is one of those combinations that sounds fancy and is actually one of the simplest, most protein-rich ways to start the day.

**Smoked Salmon Avocado Toast** — Whole grain toast with mashed avocado, a few slices of smoked salmon, capers, thinly sliced red onion, fresh dill, and a squeeze of lemon. A breakfast that delivers healthy fat, protein, and fiber in five minutes with no cooking required. The combination of avocado and smoked salmon is one of the most naturally complementary flavor pairings in the kitchen.

**Salmon and Egg Scramble** — Leftover baked salmon or canned salmon flaked into a pan with sautéed spinach and onion, then scrambled eggs folded in and cooked gently. Season with fresh dill and lemon. A high-protein breakfast that uses last night's leftover salmon and takes ten minutes.

**Salmon Breakfast Bowls** — Warm quinoa or brown rice topped with a piece of pan-seared salmon, sliced avocado, a poached egg, cherry tomatoes, and fresh herbs with a drizzle of olive oil. A complete, satisfying breakfast that covers protein, healthy fat, and fiber in one bowl.

**Breakfast Burritos with Salmon** — Smoked salmon, scrambled eggs, spinach, avocado, and a little cream cheese in a whole wheat tortilla. A breakfast burrito that feels more elevated than the standard version and delivers significantly more omega-3s.

**Salmon Frittata** — Flaked cooked or smoked salmon layered into a skillet with sautéed spinach, onion, and fresh dill, then covered with whisked eggs and finished in the oven at 375°F until set and golden. A frittata that holds in the refrigerator for four days and works for breakfast, lunch, or dinner.

**Cottage Cheese Toast with Salmon** — Whole grain toast topped with a generous spread of cottage cheese, smoked salmon, cucumber, fresh dill, and a squeeze of lemon. A high-protein, no-cook breakfast that comes together in two minutes and tastes considerably more sophisticated than the effort required.

**Spinach and Salmon Skillet** — Fresh spinach wilted in olive oil with garlic, then flaked cooked salmon added and warmed through. Serve alongside eggs or over whole grain toast. A fast, nutrient-dense breakfast that uses leftover salmon beautifully.

## Lunch

Salmon makes some of the most satisfying and elegant lunch dishes — preparations that feel more intentional than a standard midday meal and hold up well for meal prep.

**Salmon Caesar Salad** — Romaine with a lightened Caesar dressing made from Greek yogurt and lemon, topped with a pan-seared or baked salmon fillet, shaved parmesan, and whole grain croutons. A lunch that feels like a restaurant salad and comes together in 15 minutes if the salmon is already cooked.

**Mediterranean Salmon Bowls** — Cooked salmon over a base of quinoa or farro with cucumber, cherry tomatoes, kalamata olives, feta, red onion, and a lemon-herb dressing. A complete lunch that covers protein, healthy fat, and fiber in one colorful bowl.

**Salmon Wraps** — Flaked cooked or canned salmon with avocado, mixed greens, cucumber, red onion, and a Greek yogurt dill dressing in a whole wheat tortilla. A portable lunch that travels well and holds together without getting soggy.

**Salmon Pasta Salad** — Whole grain or chickpea pasta cooled and tossed with flaked salmon, cherry tomatoes, cucumber, olives, capers, fresh herbs, and a lemon vinaigrette. A meal prep lunch that improves as it sits and holds well for three days.

**Salmon Tacos** — Grilled or baked salmon flaked into corn tortillas with shredded cabbage slaw, avocado, fresh salsa, and a drizzle of Greek yogurt lime sauce. A lighter, fresher taco that delivers more protein and omega-3s than a standard beef or chicken version.

**Salmon Sandwiches** — Canned salmon mixed with Greek yogurt, Dijon, celery, red onion, and lemon — essentially a higher-protein version of tuna salad — served on whole grain bread with lettuce and tomato. A fast lunch from pantry staples that's genuinely better than most people expect canned salmon to be.

**Spinach Salads with Salmon** — Baby spinach with pan-seared salmon, sliced strawberries or mandarin oranges, toasted walnuts, red onion, and a simple balsamic vinaigrette. A lunch salad with enough protein and fat from the salmon and walnuts to be genuinely filling.

## Dinner

Salmon at dinner is where it does its most impressive work — quick enough for a Tuesday night and good enough for a dinner party Saturday.

**Lemon Garlic Baked Salmon** — Salmon fillets seasoned with olive oil, minced garlic, lemon zest and juice, fresh herbs, salt, and pepper, roasted at 400°F for 12 to 15 minutes depending on thickness. The most straightforward salmon preparation and one of the best. The garlic and lemon do exactly what's needed without overcomplicating what is already a flavorful ingredient.

**Honey Glazed Salmon** — Salmon fillets brushed with a glaze of honey, Dijon, garlic, and a splash of low-sodium soy sauce, then roasted or pan-seared until caramelized. The glaze creates a sweet, savory crust that contrasts beautifully with the richness of the fish. A dinner that takes 20 minutes and tastes like it didn't.

**Teriyaki Salmon** — Salmon fillets marinated in a simple teriyaki sauce of low-sodium soy sauce, honey, garlic, and ginger, then pan-seared or grilled until glazed and slightly caramelized. Serve over brown rice with steamed broccoli and sliced green onions. A complete dinner that covers protein, vegetables, and whole grain in one tidy bowl.

**Sheet Pan Salmon and Vegetables** — Salmon fillets and whatever vegetables are in the refrigerator — asparagus, cherry tomatoes, zucchini, broccoli, or bell peppers — tossed with olive oil and seasoning and roasted together on one sheet pan at 400°F for 15 to 20 minutes. A complete dinner from a single pan with almost no cleanup. The vegetables roast alongside the salmon and absorb its juices.

**Salmon Pasta** — Flaked cooked or canned salmon tossed with whole grain pasta, garlic, olive oil, capers, cherry tomatoes, fresh parsley, and a squeeze of lemon. A fast pasta dinner that comes together in 20 minutes and tastes like something from a coastal Italian restaurant.

**Salmon Alfredo** — Cooked salmon over whole grain pasta in a sauce made from blended cottage cheese, parmesan, garlic, and a little pasta water. The cottage cheese delivers the creaminess of a traditional Alfredo with significantly more protein and less saturated fat. A comfort food dinner that delivers on every front.

**Salmon Rice Bowls** — Pan-seared or baked salmon over brown or cauliflower rice with edamame, shredded carrot, cucumber, avocado, and a drizzle of sesame-ginger dressing. A deconstructed sushi bowl that's one of the most popular weeknight dinners for a reason — fast, fresh, and genuinely satisfying.

**Grilled Salmon** — Salmon fillets brushed with olive oil, seasoned generously, and grilled over medium-high heat for four to five minutes per side. The grill adds a smokiness that complements salmon's natural richness in a way that oven roasting doesn't quite achieve. Serve with grilled vegetables and a simple herb sauce.

**Salmon Cakes** — Canned or leftover cooked salmon mixed with egg, oats or whole wheat breadcrumbs, Dijon, lemon, fresh herbs, and diced onion, formed into patties and pan-fried until golden and crispy. Serve with Greek yogurt tartar sauce and a simple green salad. A dinner that uses pantry staples and comes together in 20 minutes.

**Creamy Salmon Chowder** — Salmon fillets simmered in a broth of potato, onion, celery, garlic, and corn, finished with a small amount of light cream or blended cottage cheese for body. A warming, deeply satisfying soup that works as a complete dinner and reheats beautifully.

**Mediterranean Salmon** — Salmon fillets baked on a bed of cherry tomatoes, olives, capers, red onion, garlic, and olive oil at 400°F for 15 minutes. The tomatoes and olives create a built-in sauce as they roast around the fish. A one-dish dinner that looks impressive and requires almost no effort.

**Salmon Burgers** — Fresh salmon finely chopped or processed and mixed with green onion, Dijon, garlic, lemon, and a little egg, formed into patties and pan-seared or grilled. Serve on a whole grain bun with avocado, arugula, and Greek yogurt remoulade. A burger that delivers more omega-3s in one meal than most people get in a week.

**Air Fryer Salmon** — Salmon fillets seasoned with olive oil, garlic, and herbs, air-fried at 400°F for 8 to 10 minutes until the outside is slightly crispy and the center is just cooked through. One of the fastest and most reliable ways to cook salmon — minimal cleanup, consistent results, ready in under 15 minutes.

## Side Dishes

Salmon pairs naturally with sides that complement its richness without competing with it — vegetables, whole grains, and fresh preparations that let the fish remain the focus.

Roasted broccoli with garlic and lemon is one of the most natural pairings — the bitterness of the broccoli cuts through the richness of the salmon. Garlic green beans sautéed in olive oil take ten minutes and go with almost any salmon preparation. Quinoa cooked in broth with fresh herbs absorbs any sauce or glaze from the salmon and becomes part of the dish. Brown rice and wild rice both work beautifully under salmon in a bowl or alongside it on a plate. Roasted asparagus with olive oil and parmesan is the classic salmon accompaniment and continues to be one of the best. Sweet potatoes roasted until caramelized add natural sweetness that contrasts the savory fish. Brussels sprouts roasted until crispy provide the same contrast with more bitterness.

## Snacks and Appetizers

**Smoked Salmon Crostini** — Thin whole grain toasts or cucumber rounds topped with a spread of cream cheese or herbed cottage cheese, a slice of smoked salmon, capers, fresh dill, and a squeeze of lemon. An elegant appetizer that requires no cooking and impresses every time.

**Salmon Dip** — Smoked or canned salmon blended with Greek yogurt cream cheese, lemon, fresh dill, capers, and red onion. Serve with whole grain crackers and sliced vegetables. A protein-rich dip that works as a party appetizer or a snack and comes together in five minutes.

**Cucumber Salmon Bites** — Thick cucumber slices topped with a dollop of herbed cream cheese and a small piece of smoked salmon, garnished with fresh dill. A two-bite appetizer that's fresh, elegant, and requires no cooking at all.

**Salmon Pinwheels** — Whole wheat tortillas spread with herbed cream cheese, layered with smoked salmon, spinach, cucumber, and red onion, rolled tightly and sliced into rounds. A party appetizer that can be assembled ahead and refrigerated until ready to serve.

**Mini Salmon Cakes** — Small versions of salmon cakes made from canned salmon, egg, herbs, and oats, pan-fried until golden. Serve with Greek yogurt dip and fresh herbs on a platter. An appetizer that doubles as a light dinner.

**Lettuce Wraps** — Flaked seasoned salmon or smoked salmon with diced cucumber, avocado, green onion, and a sesame-ginger dressing, spooned into butter lettuce leaves. A fresh, light appetizer that works as a starter or a snack.

## How to Choose and Store Salmon

Atlantic salmon is the most widely available and has a rich, buttery flavor that works in almost any preparation — it's the most forgiving for beginners because its higher fat content means it's harder to overcook. Sockeye salmon is leaner with a deeper, more intense flavor and vibrant red color — it cooks faster and benefits from shorter cooking times. Coho salmon is mild and versatile, a middle ground between Atlantic and Sockeye. Canned salmon is an underutilized pantry staple — it's just as nutritious as fresh, requires no cooking, and works in salmon cakes, salads, sandwiches, pasta, and dips.

Fresh salmon should be used within one to two days of purchase. Store in the coldest part of the refrigerator, ideally on a plate or in a container over ice. Cooked salmon keeps in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to three days. Raw salmon freezes well — wrap individual fillets tightly and freeze for up to three months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator before cooking. Cooked salmon, salmon cakes, and salmon chowder also freeze well and reheat without significant quality loss.

## Don't Let Salmon Go to Waste

Leftover cooked salmon is genuinely versatile. Flake it into a grain bowl with whatever is in the refrigerator. Mix it with Greek yogurt and lemon for a quick salmon salad for sandwiches or wraps. Add it to scrambled eggs. Fold it into pasta with garlic and olive oil. Make salmon cakes. Add it to a frittata. Stir it into a soup in the last few minutes. A single salmon fillet can become two or three different meals throughout the week — which makes cooking a larger piece than needed at dinner one of the most practical meal prep moves available.

## FAQ

**What's the best way to cook salmon for beginners?**
Oven roasting is the most forgiving method. Season the fillet with olive oil, salt, pepper, garlic, and lemon, and roast at 400°F for 12 to 15 minutes depending on thickness. The salmon is done when it flakes easily with a fork and the center is just opaque. It's difficult to ruin salmon in the oven, which makes it an ideal starting point.

**How do I know when salmon is cooked through?**
The flesh should change from translucent dark pink to opaque lighter pink and flake easily when pressed with a fork. The center can remain very slightly translucent for a more tender result — fully cooked through is not the only acceptable outcome. Internal temperature of 125°F to 130°F for medium, 145°F for fully cooked.

**Is canned salmon as nutritious as fresh?**
Yes — canned salmon retains its omega-3 fatty acids, protein, and most vitamins through the canning process. Wild-caught canned salmon (sockeye is common) is particularly nutritious. Look for cans packed in water rather than oil and choose brands with the skin and bones included if possible — the bones are soft enough to eat and add significant calcium.

**Is salmon good for people on GLP-1 medications?**
Salmon is excellent for anyone managing appetite or portions. The combination of high-quality protein and omega-3 fatty acids creates genuine satiety that lasts well past the meal. It's one of the most filling proteins available relative to its caloric content, and the healthy fats support overall metabolic health.

**Can I cook salmon from frozen?**
Yes — salmon can be roasted directly from frozen at 400°F for 20 to 25 minutes rather than the standard 12 to 15. Rinse the fillet under cold water first to remove any ice crystals, pat dry, season, and roast as usual for slightly longer. The result is slightly less even than thawed salmon but works well for weeknight cooking.
