Black beans are one of the most reliable things you can keep in your kitchen. Open a can, rinse them off, and you're already halfway to dinner. They're filling, versatile, and they carry flavor from whatever you cook them in — which means they belong in more meals than most people realize.

If you've got black beans and you're wondering what to make, you've landed in the right place.

---

## Why Black Beans Deserve More Credit

Most people think of black beans as a taco topping or a side dish at a Mexican restaurant. That's fair — but it's also barely scratching the surface.

Black beans are one of the most protein-dense plant foods available, delivering around 15 grams of protein per cooked cup. They're also loaded with fiber, iron, folate, magnesium, potassium, and antioxidants — and they take on seasoning beautifully.

For anyone eating in a way that prioritizes satiety — whether that's managing portions, supporting GLP-1 medication, or just trying to feel satisfied without overeating — black beans are a practical, filling foundation for real meals.

## 25 Ways to Use Black Beans

### 1. Black Bean Tacos

Skip the ground beef and use seasoned black beans as your taco base. Warm them in a pan with cumin, garlic powder, smoked paprika, and a splash of lime juice until the liquid cooks off and the beans are slightly crispy on the outside. Load them into corn tortillas with shredded cabbage, pico de gallo, and a drizzle of Greek yogurt in place of sour cream.

The texture is heartier than you'd expect, and the flavor hits all the same notes as a classic taco night.

### 2. Black Bean Chili

Black beans hold up beautifully in chili. Use them alongside ground turkey or on their own with extra vegetables — diced tomatoes, corn, zucchini, and kidney beans. The key is layering spice early: toast your cumin and chili powder in the oil before anything else hits the pan.

Serve with a small spoonful of Greek yogurt on top and fresh scallions. It's one of those meals that tastes like it took all day.

### 3. Burrito Bowls

The weeknight workhorse. Brown rice or cauliflower rice on the bottom, seasoned black beans, roasted corn, sliced peppers, salsa, avocado, and whatever else you've got in the fridge. It's fast, it's filling, and it's different every time depending on what you have — and it's one of the easiest meals to prep in advance.

### 4. Burritos

Seasoned black beans as the anchor filling with rice, cheese, salsa, and sautéed peppers and onions. Wrap tight, cook seam-side down in a dry pan for a minute to seal, and you have a complete meal that travels well and reheats even better.

### 5. Black Bean Quesadillas

Mash black beans with a little lime juice and spread them on a whole wheat tortilla like a paste. Add cheese, caramelized onions, and roasted peppers, then fold and cook in a dry pan until golden and crispy on both sides. The mashed beans act as both filling and binder — your quesadilla holds together better and you get protein in every bite.

### 6. Southwest Salad

Romaine or mixed greens topped with black beans, roasted corn, diced avocado, cherry tomatoes, red onion, and a lime-cumin vinaigrette. Add grilled chicken or leave it as-is for a complete vegetarian meal. The beans make it filling enough to be a real lunch.

### 7. Black Bean Dip

Blend black beans with garlic, lime juice, cumin, a little olive oil, and a pinch of salt until smooth. It's a faster, higher-protein alternative to hummus that works just as well with vegetables, crackers, or pita. Add roasted red pepper or chipotle in adobo if you want it smokier.

### 8. Black Bean Burgers

Homemade black bean burgers beat the store-bought version every time. Mash two cans of drained black beans with sautéed onion and garlic, an egg, a handful of oats or breadcrumbs, and your seasoning of choice. Form into patties, chill for 30 minutes, then pan-fry or bake until they hold together and develop a crust.

### 9. Black Bean Brownies

This is the one that surprises everyone. Blend a can of drained black beans with eggs, cocoa powder, a little oil, sugar, vanilla, and a pinch of salt. Bake at 350°F for about 25 minutes. The result is dense, fudgy, and genuinely good — and no one will guess the ingredient unless you tell them. The beans replace the flour entirely and add fiber and protein to every square.

### 10. Taco Soup

Everything you love about tacos in a one-pot meal. Brown ground turkey or use beans only, then add black beans, kidney beans, corn, diced tomatoes, green chiles, broth, and taco seasoning. Simmer for 20 minutes. Top with a small amount of shredded cheese, Greek yogurt, and sliced scallions.

### 11. Enchilada Casserole

Layer corn tortillas with black beans, enchilada sauce, sautéed peppers and onions, and cheese. Repeat the layers, finish with sauce and cheese on top, and bake until bubbling. It's faster than rolling individual enchiladas and just as satisfying.

### 12. Breakfast Hash

Dice and sauté potatoes with onion and bell pepper until crispy, then stir in black beans for the last few minutes. Season with cumin and smoked paprika and top with a fried egg. The beans stretch the potatoes further and turn a simple breakfast into something that keeps you full all morning.

### 13. Stuffed Sweet Potatoes

Bake sweet potatoes until tender, then split and fill with warm seasoned black beans, corn, salsa, and a drizzle of Greek yogurt. The combination of sweet potato and savory spiced beans is one of those meals that sounds simple and tastes like you put real effort in.

### 14. Corn and Black Bean Salsa

Mix black beans with roasted or fresh corn, diced red onion, jalapeño, cilantro, lime juice, and a pinch of cumin. Serve as a dip, spoon it over grilled chicken or fish, or use it as a taco topping. It comes together in five minutes and works on almost everything.

### 15. Pasta Salad

Add black beans to cold pasta salad with roasted corn, cherry tomatoes, red onion, and a cilantro-lime vinaigrette. The beans add protein and make the salad filling enough to work as a main dish rather than just a side.

### 16. Stuffed Zucchini Boats

Halve zucchini lengthwise, scoop out the center, and fill with a mixture of black beans, diced tomatoes, corn, and cheese. Bake at 375°F for about 25 minutes. The filling is hearty and flavorful, and the zucchini holds everything together.

### 17. Stuffed Bell Peppers

Halve bell peppers and fill with black beans, cooked rice, diced tomatoes, corn, and spices. Top with a small amount of shredded cheese and bake at 375°F for about 25 minutes until the peppers are tender. A complete meal in its own edible container.

### 18. Grain Bowls

Farro, quinoa, or brown rice as the base, topped with seasoned black beans, roasted vegetables, avocado, and whatever sauce you have — tahini, salsa verde, or a simple lime vinaigrette. This is the template that works with whatever's in your fridge.

### 19. Vegetable Soup

Add a can of black beans to any vegetable soup for protein and body. They absorb the broth flavor completely and make a light soup filling enough to be a meal. Works especially well in tomato-based soups with greens.

### 20. Black Bean Patties

Similar to burgers but smaller — formed into patties and served without a bun over salad, in wraps, or alongside roasted vegetables. Easier to portion and just as satisfying as the full burger version.

### 21. Lettuce Wraps

Spoon seasoned black beans with corn, diced avocado, and salsa into large romaine or butter lettuce leaves. Add a drizzle of lime crema (Greek yogurt, lime juice, garlic) and fold. Fast, fresh, and genuinely filling.

### 22. Southwest Pizza

Use a whole wheat crust or flatbread, spread with salsa instead of marinara, and top with black beans, corn, diced peppers, red onion, and a modest amount of cheese. Bake until bubbly. Finish with sliced avocado and cilantro after it comes out of the oven.

### 23. Wraps

Warm a whole wheat tortilla and fill it with black beans, shredded lettuce, diced tomato, avocado, and salsa. It's a five-minute lunch that's more filling than it looks.

### 24. Mexican Casserole

Layer tortilla chips, black beans, seasoned ground turkey or beef, salsa, and cheese in a baking dish. Bake until everything melds together and the top is golden. It's the kind of dish that gets requested again.

### 25. Mexican Fried Rice

Cook rice, then stir-fry it in a hot pan with black beans, corn, diced peppers, cumin, and chili powder. Finish with lime juice and cilantro. It's faster than ordering takeout and uses up leftover rice perfectly.

## Canned vs. Dried Black Beans

Both are nutritious — the choice is about convenience and cost.

**Canned** beans are ready in minutes. Rinse them before using to reduce sodium and improve flavor. They're the right call for weeknight cooking when time is short.

**Dried** beans are more economical and freeze beautifully after cooking. Cook a large batch on the weekend, portion into freezer bags, and you have ready-to-use beans all week. One pound of dried beans yields about the same as three cans.

## How to Make Black Beans Taste Better

**Rinse canned beans.** A 30-second rinse removes excess sodium and brightens the flavor immediately.

**Season while they cook.** Black beans absorb salt and spices as they heat — add seasoning early, not at the end.

**Acid at the finish.** A squeeze of lime juice right before serving wakes up everything.

**Toast your spices.** Toast cumin and chili powder in a dry pan before adding beans — the flavor deepens significantly.

**Don't skip the sauté.** Even a brief cook in olive oil with garlic and onion transforms canned beans from flat to flavorful.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I substitute dried black beans for canned?

Yes. One 15-ounce can equals about 1.5 cups of cooked dried beans. Dried beans require soaking and a longer cook time but are significantly cheaper and let you control the sodium.

### Are black beans a good protein source?

Yes — around 15 grams of protein per cooked cup. They're also high in fiber, which means the protein and carbohydrates digest more slowly, keeping you fuller longer.

### Do black beans freeze well?

They do. Cooked or drained canned beans freeze flat in zip-lock bags for up to three months. Add frozen directly to soups and stews without thawing.

### What spices go best with black beans?

Cumin and garlic are the core two. From there: smoked paprika, chili powder, oregano, coriander, and cayenne. Lime juice and fresh cilantro at the end make everything better.

### Can I eat black beans every day?

Most people can. They're highly nutritious and the fiber supports digestive health. If you're not used to eating a lot of fiber, increase them gradually and drink plenty of water.

### How does Old2New use black beans in recipe transformations?

Old2New frequently suggests substituting black beans for some or all of the ground meat in chili, tacos, and casseroles, or blending them into brownie batter in place of flour. The specific use depends on your health goals and the recipe you bring to it.
