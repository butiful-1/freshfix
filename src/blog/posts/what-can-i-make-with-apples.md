# What Can I Make with Apples?

When most people think about cooking with apples, they think pie. And pie is great — but it's also about one percent of what this ingredient can do.

Apples add natural sweetness, crunch, fiber, and brightness to meals across every part of the day. They work in savory dishes just as well as sweet ones. They pair with protein, hold up in the oven, and make comfort food feel a little more intentional without changing what makes it good.

If you've got apples and you're wondering what to make, here's where to start.

---

## Why Apples Are Worth Keeping Around

Apples are naturally sweet, high in fiber, budget-friendly, easy to store, and genuinely good in both sweet and savory applications. They're one of the few fruits that work as well in a pork dish as they do in a muffin. They're also one of the easiest ingredients to work into meal prep — slice them ahead, toss with a little lemon juice, and they hold well for several days.

For anyone making shifts in how they eat — whether that's managing portions, supporting GLP-1 medication, or just trying to add more whole foods — apples are a natural addition. The fiber slows digestion, the natural sugar satisfies a sweet craving without a processed ingredient, and the versatility means you're never stuck with the same meal twice.

## Breakfast

Apples are a natural fit for breakfast because they add sweetness without relying on added sugar. The fiber also helps start the day with something that actually holds you over.

**Apple Cinnamon Overnight Oats** — Combine rolled oats, almond milk, chia seeds, cinnamon, and diced apple in a jar the night before. By morning you have a ready-to-eat breakfast that's filling, naturally sweet, and requires zero effort in the morning.

**Apple Protein Oatmeal** — Cook oats with diced apple, cinnamon, and a scoop of protein powder or a spoonful of almond butter stirred in at the end. The apple softens slightly as the oats cook and the whole thing tastes like apple pie in a bowl.

**Greek Yogurt Apple Parfaits** — Layer Greek yogurt with diced apple, a drizzle of honey, granola, and a pinch of cinnamon. It's a five-minute breakfast that delivers protein, fiber, and natural sweetness in the same bowl.

**Apple Cottage Cheese Breakfast Bowls** — Spoon cottage cheese into a bowl, top with sliced apples, a drizzle of honey, and a sprinkle of walnuts and cinnamon. High protein, naturally sweet, and ready in two minutes.

**Apple Cinnamon Pancakes** — Fold finely diced apple and cinnamon into your regular pancake batter. The apple adds moisture and sweetness that means you need far less syrup at the table.

**Apple Breakfast Muffins** — Whole wheat flour, diced apple, cinnamon, a little honey, and Greek yogurt in place of oil. They bake up moist, naturally sweet, and hold well in the fridge all week.

**Baked Apples with Cinnamon** — Core apples, fill with oats, cinnamon, a little honey and coconut oil, and bake at 375°F for 25 minutes. They come out tender and caramelized and work as breakfast or dessert depending on what you serve alongside.

**Apple Smoothies** — Blend apple with spinach, banana, ginger, and almond milk. The apple adds natural sweetness that balances the spinach completely — you get a green smoothie that actually tastes good.

**Apple Peanut Butter Toast** — Whole grain toast with peanut or almond butter and thin apple slices on top. Add a drizzle of honey and a pinch of cinnamon. It's one of those combinations that sounds too simple and tastes better than it has any right to.

**Apple Chia Pudding** — Blend apple with almond milk and mix into chia seeds with cinnamon. Refrigerate overnight. It thickens into a creamy, fiber-rich pudding that works as breakfast or a snack.

## Lunch

Fresh apple adds crunch and brightness to lunches in a way that makes ordinary salads and wraps feel more complete.

**Apple Chicken Salad** — Diced apple, shredded chicken, celery, red onion, Greek yogurt instead of mayo, and a little lemon juice and Dijon. It's a chicken salad that tastes fresher and lighter than the classic version and works on bread, over greens, or in a wrap.

**Turkey Apple Wraps** — Sliced turkey, thin apple slices, spinach, sharp cheddar, and a little honey mustard in a whole wheat tortilla. The apple adds crunch that makes this feel more substantial than a standard wrap.

**Spinach Apple Walnut Salad** — Baby spinach with sliced apple, toasted walnuts, red onion, crumbled goat cheese or feta, and a simple apple cider vinaigrette. It's one of those salads that converts people who claim they don't like salad.

**Apple Quinoa Bowls** — Cooked quinoa with diced apple, dried cranberries, toasted pecans, and a lemon-honey dressing. Works warm or cold and holds well for meal prep.

**Apple Slaw** — Shredded cabbage and julienned apple with a light apple cider vinegar dressing. A brighter, more interesting alternative to traditional coleslaw that works alongside sandwiches, tacos, or grilled protein.

**Apple Grilled Cheese** — Thin apple slices inside a grilled cheese with sharp cheddar on whole grain bread. The apple adds sweetness and crunch that makes this feel like a completely different sandwich.

**Apple and Cottage Cheese Plate** — Sliced apples alongside cottage cheese with a drizzle of honey and a handful of walnuts. Fast, filling, no cooking required.

## Dinner

This is where most people are surprised. Apples in savory dinner dishes work because their sweetness balances richness and their texture holds up to heat.

**Pork Tenderloin with Apples** — Sear pork tenderloin and roast alongside sliced apples, onions, fresh thyme, and a little apple cider. The apples caramelize and create a natural sauce that doesn't need any thickening. One of the best simple dinner combinations there is.

**Apple Stuffed Chicken Breast** — Butterfly chicken breasts and stuff with a mixture of diced apple, sage, shallots, and a little goat cheese. Bake until golden. The filling keeps the chicken moist and the flavors are genuinely impressive for how simple the technique is.

**Apple Turkey Meatballs** — Ground turkey meatballs with finely grated apple mixed into the meat. The apple adds moisture and a subtle sweetness that makes these more tender than standard turkey meatballs, which tend to dry out.

**Chicken Apple Sausage Skillet** — Chicken apple sausage with sautéed peppers, onions, and diced apple over brown rice or farro. A complete one-pan meal in under 20 minutes.

**Apple Wild Rice Pilaf** — Cooked wild rice with diced apple, dried cranberries, toasted pecans, and fresh herbs. Works as a side dish alongside any protein or as a standalone grain bowl.

**Apple Roasted Vegetables** — Add quartered apple to a sheet pan of root vegetables — carrots, parsnips, sweet potatoes — with olive oil and rosemary. The apple caramelizes at the edges and adds a sweetness that makes the whole pan more interesting.

**Apple Brussels Sprouts** — Roast Brussels sprouts with sliced apple, bacon or turkey bacon, and a drizzle of balsamic. The apple balances the bitterness of the sprouts and the whole dish converts Brussels sprout skeptics.

**Apple Cranberry Stuffing** — Add diced apple and dried cranberries to your stuffing base. The apple adds moisture and sweetness that lifts the whole dish and reduces how much butter you need to get the same richness.

## Snacks

Apples are one of the best snack ingredients because they pair naturally with protein — which means they keep you full instead of just giving you a quick sugar hit.

**Apple with Peanut Butter** — The classic. Still one of the best snack combinations for fiber and protein in one easy package.

**Apple and Almond Butter** — Same concept with a slightly more complex flavor. Add a pinch of cinnamon and sea salt on top.

**Cottage Cheese with Apples** — A scoop of cottage cheese with sliced apple and honey. High protein, naturally sweet, ready in one minute.

**Homemade Apple Chips** — Thinly slice apples, toss with cinnamon, and bake at 225°F for two hours until crispy. Better than store-bought and no added sugar.

**Apple Trail Mix** — Dried apple pieces with almonds, walnuts, pumpkin seeds, and a few dark chocolate chips. A portable snack that travels well and actually satisfies.

**Cinnamon Apple Slices** — Slice apples, sprinkle with cinnamon, and microwave for 60 seconds. You get the warm, soft texture of baked apple in under two minutes.

## Healthier Desserts

This is where apples really shine — they satisfy a sweet craving while bringing fiber and natural sugar instead of processed ingredients.

**Healthier Apple Crisp** — Sliced apples topped with an oat, almond flour, cinnamon, and coconut oil crumble. Bake at 350°F for 35 minutes. It has all the comfort of the original with significantly better ingredients.

**Baked Cinnamon Apples** — Halved apples baked with cinnamon, a little maple syrup, and a sprinkle of oats. Serve with a spoonful of Greek yogurt. It's a dessert that takes 30 minutes and feels completely indulgent.

**Apple Oat Bars** — Rolled oats, mashed apple, cinnamon, honey, and a little almond butter pressed into a pan and baked. They hold together well, travel easily, and work as dessert or a snack.

**Apple Protein Muffins** — Whole wheat flour, Greek yogurt, diced apple, protein powder, cinnamon, and a little honey. Moist, filling, and genuinely good enough that you don't feel like you're eating something "healthy."

**Apple Cheesecake Cups** — Greek yogurt cream cheese with honey and vanilla spooned into small cups and topped with cinnamon-spiced diced apple. A lighter take on cheesecake that requires no baking.

**Apple Yogurt Bark** — Spread Greek yogurt on a parchment-lined sheet, top with thin apple slices, granola, cinnamon, and a drizzle of honey, then freeze for two hours. Break into pieces. A freezer snack that works as dessert.

**Apple Cinnamon Energy Balls** — Rolled oats, almond butter, honey, dried apple pieces, cinnamon, and a little vanilla rolled into balls and refrigerated. A two-bite dessert with real ingredients.

## Apples as a Recipe Transformation Tool

Some of the most beloved comfort food recipes in any family collection involve apples. Old2New can help transform them with ingredient swaps that keep the flavor and comfort while making them fit better into how you eat now:

- **Apple pie** — swap refined flour crust for an oat and almond flour version, reduce sugar and let the apple's natural sweetness carry more of the load
- **Apple crisp** — replace butter-heavy topping with an oat and coconut oil crumble
- **Apple muffins** — swap white flour for whole wheat, use Greek yogurt instead of oil
- **Apple cake** — reduce sugar, add protein powder, use applesauce in place of some of the fat
- **Apple bread** — whole grain flour, less sugar, add walnuts and seeds for texture
- **Apple cinnamon rolls** — whole wheat dough, apple filling, Greek yogurt glaze instead of cream cheese frosting

The apple stays. The comfort stays. The recipe just works a little better.

## FAQ

**What are the best apples for cooking?**
For baking and savory dishes, Granny Smith holds its shape and adds tartness that balances sweetness well. Honeycrisp and Fuji work in raw applications — salads, slaws, snacks — where you want sweetness and crunch. Gala and Braeburn are good all-purpose options.

**Do I need to peel apples before cooking?**
Not always. The skin adds fiber and holds the apple together during cooking. For smooth applications like applesauce or puréed soups, peel them. For crisp, muffins, and roasted dishes, leave the skin on.

**How do I keep cut apples from browning?**
Toss with lemon or lime juice immediately after cutting. The acid slows oxidation without affecting flavor.

**Can I substitute applesauce for oil in baking?**
Yes — a 1:1 substitution works in most muffin and quick bread recipes. The result is slightly denser and moister. Start with replacing half the oil and adjust from there.

**Are apples good for people on GLP-1 medications?**
Apples are high in fiber and water content, which supports satiety and steady digestion — both useful for anyone managing appetite or portions. The natural sugar digests more slowly than processed sugar because of the fiber.
