# What Can I Make with Shrimp?

Shrimp might be the most practical protein in the kitchen. It goes from frozen to cooked in under ten minutes, it works in pasta, tacos, stir fries, grain bowls, soups, and salads with equal success, and it takes on seasoning faster and more completely than almost any other protein. Garlic butter shrimp tastes like garlic butter. Cajun shrimp tastes like Cajun spices. Teriyaki shrimp tastes like teriyaki. The shrimp itself is mild enough to let the preparation drive the dish — which means one protein works across a remarkable range of cuisines and flavor profiles.

It's also genuinely fast. A pound of shrimp takes three to four minutes in a hot pan. That's faster than boiling pasta, faster than roasting vegetables, faster than almost any other cooking task in the kitchen. On a weeknight when time is short and everyone is hungry, shrimp is the answer more often than not.

If you've got shrimp and you're wondering what to make, here's everything it can do.

---

## Why Shrimp Belongs in Every Kitchen

Shrimp is high in lean protein, naturally low in carbohydrates, rich in selenium and vitamin B12, cooks in under five minutes, and is one of the most freezer-friendly proteins available. A bag of frozen shrimp thaws in 20 minutes under cold running water or overnight in the refrigerator, which means it can go from completely frozen to dinner in under 30 minutes total. Buying peeled and deveined shrimp eliminates the only labor-intensive step and makes weeknight cooking genuinely effortless.

For anyone cooking more intentionally — whether that's managing portions, eating more whole foods, or supporting GLP-1 medication — shrimp is one of the most useful proteins available. The high protein-to-calorie ratio means it satisfies without a large portion, and the cooking speed means it's realistic for weeknights when the alternative might be processed or takeout food.

## Breakfast

Shrimp at breakfast is more common than most people in the United States realize — across coastal cuisines in Mexico, Southeast Asia, and the American South, shrimp at the morning table is completely standard. Once you try it, the combination of protein and bold flavor makes it one of the most satisfying breakfast options available.

**Shrimp Breakfast Tacos** — Sautéed shrimp seasoned with cumin, chili powder, and garlic in warm corn tortillas with scrambled eggs, avocado, fresh salsa, and a squeeze of lime. A breakfast taco that delivers more protein per serving than almost any other morning combination and comes together in ten minutes.

**Shrimp and Egg Scramble** — Shrimp cooked in olive oil with garlic and red pepper flakes, then scrambled eggs folded in and cooked gently with fresh spinach and cherry tomatoes. Season with fresh herbs and lemon. A fast, high-protein breakfast that works equally well for dinner.

**Shrimp Avocado Toast** — Whole grain toast with mashed avocado, sautéed garlic shrimp, red pepper flakes, fresh cilantro, and a squeeze of lime. The shrimp adds protein that makes avocado toast genuinely filling rather than a light snack that leaves you hungry an hour later.

**Shrimp Omelets** — Sautéed shrimp with spinach, red onion, and a little feta or goat cheese folded into eggs. A breakfast omelet that feels more like a restaurant dish than a weekday morning meal and takes 12 minutes.

**Spinach and Shrimp Skillet** — Shrimp sautéed with garlic, then fresh spinach wilted into the pan and everything finished with lemon and fresh herbs. Serve alongside eggs or over whole grain toast. A fast, nutrient-dense breakfast that uses minimal ingredients and delivers maximum protein.

**Breakfast Grain Bowls with Shrimp** — Warm quinoa or brown rice topped with sautéed shrimp, a poached egg, sliced avocado, and fresh herbs with a drizzle of olive oil and hot sauce. A complete breakfast bowl that covers protein, healthy fat, and fiber in one dish.

## Lunch

Shrimp makes some of the freshest, most satisfying lunch preparations — dishes that feel lighter than a beef or pork lunch but are just as filling because of the protein content.

**Shrimp Caesar Salad** — Romaine with a lightened Caesar dressing made from Greek yogurt and lemon, topped with grilled or sautéed shrimp, shaved parmesan, and whole grain croutons. A lunch salad that's actually filling because of the shrimp protein and works well for meal prep if the dressing is kept separate.

**Mediterranean Shrimp Bowls** — Cooked shrimp over quinoa or farro with cucumber, cherry tomatoes, kalamata olives, feta, red onion, and a lemon-herb dressing. A complete lunch that requires minimal cooking and holds well in the refrigerator for three days.

**Shrimp Wraps** — Sautéed shrimp with shredded cabbage slaw, avocado, fresh cilantro, and a drizzle of Greek yogurt lime sauce in a whole wheat tortilla. A portable lunch that travels well and holds together without getting soggy.

**Shrimp Tacos** — Grilled or sautéed shrimp in corn tortillas with shredded cabbage, mango salsa, avocado, and a squeeze of lime. A lunch taco that feels like vacation food regardless of where you're eating it.

**Shrimp Pasta Salad** — Whole grain or chickpea pasta cooled and tossed with cooked shrimp, cherry tomatoes, cucumber, olives, capers, fresh herbs, and a lemon vinaigrette. A meal prep lunch that holds well for three days and improves as the pasta absorbs the dressing.

**Shrimp Lettuce Wraps** — Seasoned sautéed shrimp with diced mango or pineapple, shredded carrot, cucumber, and a sesame-ginger dressing, spooned into butter lettuce leaves. A light, fresh lunch that requires almost no cooking and delivers a refreshing combination of flavors.

**Shrimp Quinoa Bowls** — Cooked shrimp over a base of warm quinoa with roasted vegetables, fresh herbs, and a simple lemon-tahini dressing. A complete, protein-rich lunch that holds well for meal prep and reheats quickly.

## Dinner

This is where shrimp's speed and versatility are most valuable — a dinner protein that's ready in minutes and works across an extraordinary range of dishes and flavor profiles.

**Garlic Butter Shrimp** — Shrimp cooked in a pan with olive oil, generous amounts of garlic, a little butter, white wine or broth, and fresh parsley, finished with lemon juice. One of the most flavorful things you can make in ten minutes. Serve over whole grain pasta, brown rice, or with crusty whole grain bread to scoop up the sauce.

**Shrimp Scampi** — The classic Italian-American preparation — shrimp sautéed in olive oil and garlic with white wine and lemon, finished with a little butter and fresh parsley, tossed with whole grain linguine. A restaurant dish that comes together faster at home than it takes to drive to a restaurant.

**Shrimp Stir Fry** — Shrimp cooked with broccoli, snap peas, bell peppers, and carrots in a sauce of low-sodium soy sauce, garlic, ginger, and sesame oil, served over brown rice. The shrimp cooks in the same time as the vegetables, which means everything finishes together in one pan. A complete dinner in 20 minutes.

**Shrimp Fried Rice** — Day-old brown rice stir-fried with shrimp, eggs, peas, carrots, onion, and garlic in a very hot pan with low-sodium soy sauce and sesame oil. The shrimp adds protein that makes fried rice a complete meal rather than a side dish. One of the best uses for leftover rice in the refrigerator.

**Shrimp Fajitas** — Shrimp seasoned with cumin, chili powder, garlic, and lime cooked in a very hot skillet with sliced bell peppers and onions until charred and tender. Served in corn tortillas with avocado, salsa, and Greek yogurt. A 15-minute dinner that delivers all the flavor of the classic fajita with the fastest possible protein.

**Cajun Shrimp** — Shrimp tossed with Cajun seasoning and cooked in olive oil until pink and slightly charred at the edges. Serve over brown rice with roasted corn, black beans, and fresh cilantro, or alongside roasted vegetables. Bold, satisfying, and done in eight minutes.

**Grilled Shrimp Skewers** — Large shrimp threaded on skewers, brushed with olive oil, garlic, and herbs, and grilled over high heat for two to three minutes per side. A summer dinner that works as an appetizer, a main course, or a protein addition to any grain bowl or salad.

**Sheet Pan Shrimp and Vegetables** — Large shrimp and whatever vegetables are in the refrigerator — asparagus, cherry tomatoes, zucchini, bell peppers, or broccoli — tossed with olive oil, garlic, lemon, and herbs and roasted together at 400°F for 12 minutes. A complete dinner from one pan with almost no cleanup. The shrimp and vegetables finish at the same time if cut into similar-sized pieces.

**Shrimp Curry** — Shrimp simmered in a spiced coconut milk sauce with tomatoes, onion, garlic, ginger, and spinach. Over brown rice or with warm naan. The shrimp cooks in the curry sauce in just three to four minutes, which means the whole dish comes together in 20 minutes. A warming, deeply flavorful dinner that feels more complex than its preparation requires.

**Shrimp and Grits** — Sautéed shrimp with turkey bacon, onion, bell pepper, and garlic in a light tomato and broth sauce, served over stone-ground grits cooked with a little sharp cheddar and Greek yogurt. A Southern comfort food that works beautifully with better ingredients throughout.

**Shrimp Alfredo** — Cooked shrimp over whole grain pasta in a sauce made from blended cottage cheese, parmesan, garlic, and pasta water. The cottage cheese delivers the creaminess of a traditional Alfredo with significantly more protein and less saturated fat. A comfort food dinner that comes together in 25 minutes.

**Coconut Shrimp** — Shrimp coated in shredded coconut and whole wheat breadcrumbs, baked at 425°F until golden and slightly crispy. Serve with a simple mango-lime dipping sauce made from diced mango, lime juice, and a little honey. A crowd-pleasing appetizer or dinner that delivers all the flavor of the fried version from the oven.

**Shrimp Chowder** — Shrimp added to a light chowder of potato, corn, onion, celery, garlic, and broth, finished with a small amount of light cream or blended cottage cheese for body. The shrimp cooks in the last three minutes of simmering — any longer and it becomes rubbery. A warming, satisfying soup that works as a complete dinner.

**Lemon Herb Shrimp** — Shrimp tossed with olive oil, lemon zest, garlic, fresh thyme, and red pepper flakes, roasted at 400°F for eight minutes. The simplest possible preparation and one of the best — the lemon and herbs do exactly what's needed without overcomplicating what is already a flavorful protein.

## Snacks and Appetizers

**Shrimp Cocktail** — Chilled cooked shrimp served with a homemade cocktail sauce of tomato, horseradish, lemon, and hot sauce. The classic party appetizer and one of the most high-protein, low-calorie options on any appetizer spread.

**Shrimp Ceviche** — Fresh shrimp "cooked" in lime juice with diced cucumber, tomato, red onion, jalapeño, cilantro, and avocado. Serve with baked tortilla chips or cucumber slices. A fresh, bright appetizer that requires no heat and tastes like the best version of fresh seafood.

**Cucumber Shrimp Bites** — Thick cucumber slices topped with a dollop of herbed cream cheese or cottage cheese and a single cooked shrimp, garnished with fresh dill or cilantro. A two-bite appetizer that's elegant, fresh, and requires almost no preparation.

**Shrimp Lettuce Cups** — Seasoned cooked shrimp with diced mango, shredded carrot, cucumber, and a sesame-ginger dressing spooned into butter lettuce leaves. A fresh appetizer that works equally well as a light lunch.

**Mini Shrimp Tacos** — Small corn tortillas with a single shrimp each, topped with shredded cabbage slaw, fresh salsa, and a drizzle of avocado crema. A party appetizer that's easy to assemble in batches and goes quickly.

**Shrimp Crostini** — Whole grain toasts topped with herbed ricotta or cottage cheese and a single sautéed garlic shrimp, garnished with fresh herbs and a squeeze of lemon. An elegant appetizer that comes together in 15 minutes and impresses at any gathering.

## How to Choose and Store Shrimp

Large shrimp (16/20 per pound) work best for grilling, shrimp cocktail, skewers, and sheet pan preparations where presentation matters. Medium shrimp (21/25 per pound) are the most versatile for pasta, stir fries, tacos, soups, and everyday cooking. Buying peeled and deveined shrimp saves the most time-consuming prep step and is worth the slight premium for weeknight cooking.

Fresh shrimp should smell like the ocean — clean and slightly briny, not fishy or ammonia-like. It keeps in the refrigerator for one to two days. Frozen shrimp is just as good as fresh for most applications and far more convenient — it keeps in the freezer for up to three months and thaws under cold running water in 15 to 20 minutes. Cooked shrimp stores in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to three days.

The biggest mistake with shrimp is overcooking — shrimp goes from perfectly cooked to rubbery in about 30 seconds. Cook over high heat, watch for the moment it turns from translucent grey to opaque pink and just begins to curl into a C shape, and remove from the heat immediately.

## Don't Let Shrimp Go to Waste

Leftover cooked shrimp is one of the most versatile refrigerator items available. Add it cold to a salad. Warm it briefly in a pan with garlic and butter and serve over pasta. Chop it into a grain bowl. Fold it into scrambled eggs. Add it to a soup in the last two minutes of cooking. Make shrimp tacos with whatever tortillas and vegetables are available. The mild flavor means it fits into almost any dish without clashing, and the protein content means even a small addition makes the meal more substantial.

## FAQ

**How do I thaw frozen shrimp quickly?**
Place the frozen shrimp in a colander and run cold water over them for 15 to 20 minutes, tossing occasionally. They thaw quickly because of their small size. Do not thaw in hot water or at room temperature — cold water preserves the texture. Alternatively, thaw overnight in the refrigerator.

**How do I know when shrimp is cooked?**
Shrimp is cooked when it turns from translucent grey to opaque pink and curls into a loose C shape. An O shape means it's overcooked — rubbery and dry. Remove shrimp from the heat the moment it turns opaque and the curl is C-shaped. Residual heat will finish the cooking.

**Is shrimp healthy?**
Shrimp is high in protein, low in calories and saturated fat, and rich in selenium and vitamin B12. The cholesterol content of shrimp was once a concern, but current research shows that dietary cholesterol from shrimp has minimal impact on blood cholesterol for most people. It's one of the most nutritionally efficient proteins available.

**Is shrimp good for people on GLP-1 medications?**
Shrimp is excellent for anyone managing appetite or portions. The high protein-to-calorie ratio means it satisfies with a smaller portion than many other proteins, and the cooking speed makes it realistic for weeknights when the alternative might be a less intentional choice. It's one of the most practical proteins for building satisfying, portion-appropriate meals.

**Can I use frozen shrimp straight from the bag?**
In most cases, no — thaw first for the best texture. The exception is adding frozen shrimp directly to a soup or stew where it will thaw and cook in the liquid. For sautéed, grilled, or roasted applications, thaw first so the shrimp cooks evenly and the excess water from the thawing process doesn't steam the pan and prevent browning.
