# What Can I Make with Pasta?

Pasta is one of the most universally loved comfort foods for a reason. It's fast, affordable, satisfying, and works with nearly any protein, vegetable, or sauce you have on hand. It feeds a family on a Tuesday night and impresses guests on a Saturday. It works straight from the pot and cold from the refrigerator the next day.

It also gets an undeserved bad reputation. The pasta itself is rarely the problem — it's what gets piled on top of it and how much of it gets served. A generous portion of spaghetti buried under a heavy meat sauce and a mountain of cheese is a different meal than a modest bowl of whole wheat pasta with roasted vegetables, lean protein, and a light tomato sauce. Both are pasta. Only one feels like something you had to give up something to eat.

The goal isn't to replace pasta. It's to make the pasta dishes you already love work a little better — with better additions, better sauces, and better portion awareness — so you never feel like pasta is off the table.

## Why Pasta Belongs in Every Kitchen

Pasta is budget-friendly, quick to prepare, available in dozens of shapes for different applications, easy to pair with vegetables and proteins, excellent for meal prep, and just as good cold in a salad as hot from the stove. Whole wheat pasta adds fiber and a slightly nuttier flavor. Chickpea and lentil pastas add protein and are genuinely good in most applications — not just passable substitutes. High-protein pasta options have expanded significantly and are worth exploring if more protein per serving is a priority.

The shape matters more than most people realize. Long pasta like spaghetti and linguine works best with olive oil-based sauces and smooth tomato sauces that coat the noodle. Short pasta like penne and rigatoni holds chunky meat and vegetable sauces in its ridges and tubes. Rotini and farfalle work best in salads and lighter preparations. Elbow macaroni is built for mac and cheese and soups. Using the right shape for the application makes the dish noticeably better.

## Breakfast

Pasta at breakfast sounds unusual until you realize that leftover pasta is one of the most useful things in the refrigerator on a weekday morning.

**Breakfast Pasta Skillet** — Leftover spaghetti or penne cooked in a hot skillet with olive oil, garlic, and whatever vegetables are on hand, then topped with a fried egg. The pasta gets slightly crispy at the edges and the egg yolk becomes the sauce. A five-minute breakfast from last night's dinner.

**Egg and Spinach Pasta** — Fresh pasta or leftover pasta cooked with sautéed spinach, garlic, and a poached or fried egg on top, finished with a squeeze of lemon and fresh parmesan. A breakfast that works as well for dinner and takes ten minutes from start to finish.

**Frittata with Leftover Pasta** — Leftover pasta mixed into a whisked egg base with sautéed vegetables and cheese, poured into a skillet and finished in the oven at 375°F until set and golden. A frittata made with pasta is heartier and more filling than one without — and one of the best uses for leftover pasta in the refrigerator.

**Cottage Cheese Pasta Bowl** — Warm pasta tossed with cottage cheese, garlic, fresh herbs, and a little lemon juice until the cottage cheese melts slightly and coats the pasta in a creamy, protein-rich sauce. A breakfast bowl that delivers protein and comfort in equal measure.

**Breakfast Carbonara-Inspired Skillet** — Leftover pasta tossed in a pan with turkey bacon or chicken bacon, eggs scrambled directly into the pasta, garlic, and a little parmesan. The eggs coat the pasta in a silky, savory sauce. A breakfast that uses pantry staples and takes ten minutes.

## Lunch

Pasta makes some of the most practical and satisfying lunches — dishes that hold well for meal prep, travel easily, and taste as good cold as they do warm.

**Pasta Salad** — Rotini or farfalle cooked al dente and tossed while still warm with a vinaigrette, then combined with cherry tomatoes, cucumber, olives, red onion, fresh herbs, and whatever protein you have on hand — chickpeas, grilled chicken, or tuna. A lunch that gets better as it sits and holds in the refrigerator for four days.

**Mediterranean Pasta Salad** — Whole wheat pasta with cherry tomatoes, cucumber, kalamata olives, artichoke hearts, feta, red onion, and a lemon-olive oil dressing. One of the best pasta salads there is — bright, satisfying, and genuinely filling because of the protein from the feta and the fiber from the vegetables.

**Tuna Pasta Salad** — Elbow or rotini pasta with tuna, celery, red onion, and a dressing of Greek yogurt, Dijon, and lemon juice. A lighter version of the classic that's higher in protein and just as creamy. A meal prep lunch that holds well for three days.

**Chicken Caesar Pasta Salad** — Romaine, grilled chicken, whole grain pasta, parmesan, and a lightened Caesar dressing made with Greek yogurt and lemon. A pasta salad that eats like a complete meal and works for anyone who wants their Caesar salad to actually be filling.

**Soup with Pasta** — Small pasta shapes like ditalini or elbow macaroni added to a vegetable or chicken broth soup with vegetables, beans, and fresh herbs. The pasta absorbs the broth and adds heartiness that makes a simple vegetable soup into something more substantial. Add the pasta in the last ten minutes of cooking to prevent it from becoming too soft.

**Pasta Bowls with Roasted Vegetables** — Warm pasta tossed with whatever roasted vegetables are in the refrigerator, a drizzle of olive oil, fresh herbs, and a little parmesan. A lunch that uses up leftovers and comes together in five minutes if the vegetables are already roasted.

## Dinner

Pasta dinners are where comfort food lives — the recipes that have been on rotation in family kitchens for generations and will continue to be.

**Spaghetti with Turkey Meat Sauce** — Ground turkey browned with onion, garlic, and Italian seasoning, simmered in a rich tomato sauce and served over whole wheat spaghetti. All the comfort of spaghetti night with leaner protein and better fiber. The sauce is the part that matters — let it simmer for at least 30 minutes.

**Lasagna** — Layers of whole wheat noodles, lean turkey or beef meat sauce, part-skim ricotta, spinach, and mozzarella baked until bubbly and golden. A make-ahead dinner that feeds a crowd, freezes perfectly, and reheats beautifully. Folding spinach into the ricotta layer adds vegetables without changing anything about what makes lasagna worth eating.

**Baked Ziti** — Whole wheat penne in a tomato and turkey meat sauce with ricotta, mozzarella, and fresh basil, baked until the top is golden and bubbling. A one-dish dinner that serves six, holds in the refrigerator for four days, and freezes well in portions.

**Chicken Alfredo** — Grilled chicken over whole grain pasta in a sauce made from blended cottage cheese or Greek yogurt, garlic, parmesan, and a little pasta water. The cottage cheese version is genuinely creamy and adds significant protein while reducing the saturated fat of a traditional heavy cream Alfredo substantially.

**Pasta Primavera** — Whole grain pasta with sautéed bell peppers, zucchini, cherry tomatoes, asparagus, garlic, and olive oil, finished with fresh basil and parmesan. A summer pasta that's as light as it is colorful and comes together in 20 minutes.

**Mac and Cheese** — Elbow macaroni in a cheese sauce made with sharp cheddar, a little milk, and blended cottage cheese or Greek yogurt for extra creaminess and protein. Fold in steamed broccoli or cauliflower florets before baking. Bake until the top is golden and slightly crispy. The vegetables integrate completely and the dish is richer and more filling than the original.

**Lentil Bolognese** — Brown lentils simmered with onion, carrot, celery, garlic, canned tomatoes, and Italian herbs until the sauce is thick and meaty-textured without any meat. Over whole wheat spaghetti or pappardelle. A pasta dinner that's high in protein and fiber and one most people eat a second bowl of without realizing there's no ground beef.

**Stuffed Shells** — Jumbo pasta shells filled with a mixture of part-skim ricotta, spinach, garlic, and herbs, arranged in a baking dish, covered with marinara and a little mozzarella, and baked until bubbly. A dinner that looks impressive and can be assembled ahead and refrigerated until ready to bake.

**Creamy Mushroom Pasta** — Sliced mushrooms sautéed until deeply golden with garlic and thyme, then combined with whole grain pasta, a splash of broth, and a spoonful of blended cottage cheese or Greek yogurt for creaminess. A pasta where mushrooms do the heavy lifting — deeply flavorful, satisfying, and ready in 25 minutes.

**Shrimp Scampi** — Shrimp sautéed in olive oil with garlic, lemon, white wine or broth, and red pepper flakes, tossed with whole grain linguine and fresh parsley. A restaurant-quality pasta dinner that comes together in 15 minutes and has naturally high protein from the shrimp.

**One-Pot Pasta** — Pasta, broth, onion, garlic, cherry tomatoes, spinach, and whatever protein you have, all cooked together in one pot until the pasta is tender and has absorbed most of the liquid. The starch from the pasta thickens the remaining liquid into a silky sauce. A weeknight dinner that generates almost no cleanup.

**Taco Pasta** — Ground turkey cooked with taco seasoning and mixed with whole wheat pasta, canned tomatoes, black beans, corn, and a little cheese. A fusion comfort food that kids and adults both love and comes together in 20 minutes.

**Chili Mac** — Leftover chili or freshly made chili combined with cooked elbow macaroni and baked with a little shredded cheese on top until bubbly. One of the most satisfying uses for leftover chili and a complete dinner in one dish.

## Side Dishes

**Garlic Parmesan Pasta** — Cooked pasta tossed with olive oil, roasted garlic, fresh parmesan, and black pepper. The simplest pasta preparation and one of the best — the quality of the olive oil and parmesan matters here more than in any other dish.

**Lemon Pasta** — Cooked pasta tossed with olive oil, fresh lemon zest and juice, garlic, fresh parsley, and parmesan. Bright, simple, and a completely different dish than the garlic version — proof that pasta is as much about what you put on it as the pasta itself.

**Pesto Noodles** — Whole grain pasta tossed with homemade or good-quality store-bought pesto, a handful of cherry tomatoes, and fresh basil. A side dish that doubles as a complete meal with the addition of grilled chicken or a poached egg.

**Orzo Salad** — Orzo pasta cooked and cooled, then tossed with cucumber, cherry tomatoes, olives, feta, fresh herbs, and a lemon vinaigrette. A grain-salad-style side dish that works warm or at room temperature and pairs with any grilled protein.

**Herb Pasta** — Cooked pasta tossed with a generous amount of mixed fresh herbs — parsley, basil, chives, and dill — olive oil, lemon, and a little parmesan. A side dish that makes a simple plate of noodles feel fresh and intentional.

## Pasta as a Recipe Transformation Tool

Most of the comfort food dishes people love most are pasta-based, which means there's enormous opportunity to improve them with thoughtful swaps that keep the flavor intact. Old2New can help you transform the pasta dishes your family already makes:

- **Spaghetti and meat sauce** — swap ground beef for ground turkey or lentils, use whole wheat pasta
- **Lasagna** — add spinach to the ricotta layer, use part-skim cheese, choose whole wheat noodles
- **Mac and cheese** — blend cottage cheese into the sauce, fold in broccoli or cauliflower, use sharp cheddar so less goes further
- **Chicken Alfredo** — Greek yogurt or cottage cheese sauce instead of heavy cream
- **Baked ziti** — lean turkey, part-skim ricotta, whole wheat pasta
- **Pasta salad** — chickpea or lentil pasta for more protein, load with vegetables, use a vinaigrette instead of mayo
- **Stuffed shells** — add spinach and mushrooms to the ricotta filling, use part-skim cheese

The pasta stays. The comfort stays. The recipe just works a little better.

## How to Choose and Store Pasta

Spaghetti and linguine work best with smooth, light sauces — marinara, aglio e olio, and simple olive oil preparations. Penne and rigatoni are built for chunky sauces and baked pasta dishes where the sauce can get inside the tube. Rotini and farfalle hold dressings well and are the best choices for pasta salads. Elbow macaroni is the right call for mac and cheese and soups. Lasagna noodles, shells, and manicotti are vessels — the shape determines how the filling is contained.

Whole wheat pasta, chickpea pasta, lentil pasta, and high-protein pasta are all worth keeping on hand alongside traditional semolina pasta. The flavor and texture are slightly different but improve with quality sauces and preparations.

Store dry pasta in an airtight container in a cool, dry pantry — it keeps for over a year. Cooked pasta stores in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to four days. Toss with a small amount of olive oil before storing to prevent sticking. Pasta dishes like lasagna, baked ziti, stuffed shells, and mac and cheese freeze extremely well — portion into individual servings before freezing for convenience. Freeze sauces separately from cooked pasta when possible for the best texture on reheating.

## Don't Let Pasta Go to Waste

Leftover cooked pasta is one of the most useful things in the refrigerator. Toss it cold with vinaigrette and whatever vegetables are available for a pasta salad. Heat it in a skillet with olive oil and garlic until slightly crispy. Mix it into a frittata. Add it to a soup in the last few minutes of cooking. Bake it into a casserole with leftover sauce and cheese. The only pasta that's genuinely difficult to use up is pasta that has been sauced and sitting for more than four days — everything else is fair game.

## FAQ

**Is pasta healthy?**
Pasta is a whole food when made from semolina wheat, and it provides complex carbohydrates, some protein, and B vitamins. The nutritional profile improves with whole wheat, chickpea, or lentil versions. What typically makes pasta dishes heavy is the sauce, the portion size, and what doesn't accompany it — vegetables and lean protein make pasta a more balanced meal.

**What's the best pasta for people trying to eat more protein?**
Chickpea pasta and lentil pasta both provide significantly more protein per serving than traditional pasta — often 13 to 15 grams per serving compared to 7 to 8 grams for semolina. High-protein pasta made from enriched semolina and added protein is also widely available. Any of these work well in dishes with strong sauces where the slightly different texture is less noticeable.

**How do I prevent pasta from sticking together?**
Cook in well-salted water with enough room for the pasta to move. Stir in the first minute or two to prevent early sticking. Don't rinse the pasta after draining — the starch helps sauce adhere. If storing for later, toss with a small amount of olive oil. Add pasta to sauce immediately after draining for the best result.

**Are pasta leftovers good for meal prep?**
Yes — most pasta dishes hold well in the refrigerator for three to four days. Pasta salads are particularly good for meal prep because they improve as they sit and the pasta absorbs the dressing. Baked pasta dishes like lasagna and ziti reheat well and are arguably better on day two. Cream-based sauces separate slightly on reheating but come back together with a splash of water or broth and gentle stirring.

**Are lentil and chickpea pasta worth trying?**
Yes — particularly in dishes with bold sauces where the slightly different texture is less apparent. Chickpea pasta works well in mac and cheese, pasta salads, and dishes with tomato sauce. Lentil pasta is excellent in Bolognese and other hearty sauces. Both are worth keeping on hand as alternatives to traditional pasta.
