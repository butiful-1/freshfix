# What Can I Make with Lentils?

Lentils are one of the most underestimated ingredients in the kitchen. They sit in the pantry for months, quietly waiting, while people reach past them for something else. And that's a shame — because lentils are one of the best tools available for making comfort food more filling, more nutritious, and more affordable without changing what makes it good.

They're high in plant-based protein and fiber, which means they satisfy in a way that most vegetables don't. They absorb seasonings completely, which means they take on the flavor of whatever dish they're in rather than asserting their own. Brown lentils disappear into a chili or pasta sauce and make it heartier without anyone identifying them as a separate ingredient. Red lentils dissolve into soups and curries and create a creamy, thick texture that feels indulgent. Green and black lentils hold their shape and add substance to salads and grain bowls that actually keeps you full.

If you've got lentils and you're wondering what to make, here's where to start.

---

## Why Lentils Belong in Every Kitchen

Lentils are rich in plant-based protein, high in fiber, naturally low in fat, budget-friendly, shelf-stable for over a year, and one of the few pantry staples that require no soaking before cooking. A cup of dry lentils costs almost nothing and makes enough food for multiple meals. They cook in 20 to 30 minutes depending on the variety, freeze perfectly after cooking, and integrate into comfort food in a way that adds nutrition without subtracting anything.

For anyone cooking more intentionally — whether that's managing portions, eating more whole foods, or supporting GLP-1 medication — lentils are one of the most practical ingredients available. The combination of protein and fiber makes them exceptionally filling relative to their caloric content, which means meals built on lentils satisfy without requiring large portions.

## Breakfast

Lentils at breakfast is less common than it should be. In much of the world — particularly across the Middle East, South Asia, and the Mediterranean — savory legume-based breakfasts are completely standard. Once you try it, it makes sense.

**Savory Lentil Breakfast Bowls** — Cooked brown or green lentils seasoned with cumin, garlic, and a pinch of smoked paprika, served warm in a bowl with a poached or fried egg on top, sliced avocado, cherry tomatoes, and fresh herbs. A complete, protein-rich breakfast that holds you through the morning better than almost anything else.

**Lentil Breakfast Hash** — Cooked lentils added to a skillet with diced potato, onion, bell pepper, and garlic, cooked together until everything is slightly crispy and caramelized. The lentils add protein and fiber to a standard breakfast hash and make it genuinely filling in a way that potato alone doesn't achieve.

**Breakfast Burritos with Lentils** — Scrambled eggs, cooked seasoned lentils, black beans, salsa, and avocado in a whole wheat tortilla. The lentils add protein and texture that makes this one of the most satisfying breakfast burritos possible — and one that holds well if you make them ahead and freeze them.

**Egg and Lentil Scramble** — Cooked lentils added to a pan with sautéed onion, spinach, and garlic, then scrambled eggs folded in and cooked gently. Season with cumin, turmeric, and fresh herbs. A five-minute breakfast once the lentils are already cooked — which is why batch-cooking lentils at the start of the week pays dividends.

**Lentil Avocado Toast** — Whole grain toast with mashed avocado, a spoonful of warm seasoned lentils, a squeeze of lemon, red pepper flakes, and fresh herbs. The lentils add protein that makes avocado toast genuinely filling rather than a light snack.

**Mediterranean Breakfast Bowls** — Warm spiced lentils alongside cucumber, cherry tomatoes, kalamata olives, crumbled feta, and a drizzle of olive oil. A breakfast that tastes like a Mediterranean restaurant and comes together in minutes with precooked lentils.

## Lunch

Lentils build some of the most satisfying and practical lunches — soups, salads, and bowls that hold up well for meal prep and keep you full through the afternoon.

**Lentil Soup** — The classic. Brown or red lentils simmered with onion, carrot, celery, garlic, cumin, coriander, and vegetable or chicken broth until completely tender and slightly thickened. Red lentils dissolve into a creamy, smooth soup. Brown lentils give a heartier, more textured result. Either way, it's one of the most satisfying soups possible from pantry staples alone. A squeeze of lemon at the end is not optional.

**Lentil Salad** — Green or black lentils cooked until just tender and still holding their shape, tossed warm with a Dijon vinaigrette, roasted vegetables, fresh herbs, and a little crumbled goat cheese or feta. A salad that's actually filling because of the protein content, and one that holds well in the refrigerator for three days without the greens wilting.

**Mediterranean Lentil Bowls** — Cooked lentils over a base of mixed greens with roasted red pepper, cucumber, olives, cherry tomatoes, feta, and a lemon-herb dressing. A complete lunch that covers protein, fiber, and healthy fat in one bowl without any meat required.

**Lentil Tacos** — Seasoned brown lentils cooked with taco spices, garlic, onion, and a little tomato paste, served in corn tortillas with shredded cabbage, salsa, avocado, and a squeeze of lime. A plant-based taco that's genuinely satisfying and one most people eat without noticing there's no meat.

**Stuffed Peppers for Lunch** — Halved bell peppers filled with a mixture of cooked lentils, quinoa, diced tomatoes, and herbs, served warm or at room temperature. A portable, self-contained lunch that works well for meal prep.

**Lentil Wraps** — Cooked seasoned lentils with hummus, shredded cabbage, roasted vegetables, and fresh herbs in a whole wheat tortilla. A plant-forward wrap that holds together well and travels easily.

**Mason Jar Salads** — Layered mason jar salads with dressing on the bottom, cooked lentils, roasted vegetables, greens, and fresh herbs. The lentils sit above the dressing and stay separate until you're ready to eat. A meal prep lunch that actually improves overnight as the lentils absorb the dressing slightly.

## Dinner

This is where lentils show their most impressive range — from a hearty Bolognese to a slow-simmered stew to a vegetable curry that satisfies completely without meat.

**Lentil Chili** — Brown lentils simmered with kidney beans, black beans, canned tomatoes, onion, peppers, garlic, and chili spices. A one-pot dinner that makes six servings, gets better on day two, and freezes perfectly. The lentils add body and protein that makes this chili as hearty as any meat version.

**Lentil Bolognese** — Brown lentils cooked with onion, carrot, celery, garlic, canned tomatoes, red wine or balsamic vinegar, and Italian herbs until the sauce is thick and rich. Over whole wheat pasta. It's a pasta sauce where the lentils provide the same texture and heartiness as ground beef — and most people eating it can't identify what makes it so satisfying.

**Lentil Shepherd's Pie** — Cooked lentils with onion, carrot, peas, garlic, and a savory broth gravy, topped with mashed potato or a cauliflower-potato blend and baked until golden. A complete comfort food dinner that requires no meat and sacrifices nothing in terms of satisfaction.

**Lentil Sloppy Joes** — Seasoned brown lentils cooked with onion, bell pepper, garlic, tomato sauce, Worcestershire, and a little mustard and brown sugar until thick and saucy. Served on whole grain buns. All the sloppy, sweet-and-savory comfort of the original with a fraction of the saturated fat and significantly more fiber.

**Lentil and Vegetable Curry** — Red lentils simmered in a spiced coconut milk and tomato sauce with spinach, chickpeas, and fresh ginger until the lentils have completely dissolved and thickened the sauce. Over brown rice or with warm naan. One of the most satisfying meatless dinners in any repertoire — the red lentils create a creamy, thick curry sauce that's deeply flavorful.

**Lentil Stuffed Peppers** — Bell peppers filled with a mixture of cooked lentils, brown rice or quinoa, diced tomatoes, garlic, and Italian seasoning, topped with a little cheese and baked at 375°F for 35 minutes. A complete dinner in one vessel that works for meal prep and reheats well.

**Meatballs with Lentils** — Finely cooked lentils blended into a ground turkey or beef meatball mixture. The lentils add moisture, extend the meat, and contribute protein and fiber without being identifiable in the finished meatball. The result is a juicier, more substantial meatball that goes further per pound of meat.

**Lentil Soup for Dinner** — The same lentil soup that works for lunch is equally at home as a dinner — especially with a slice of whole grain bread and a simple salad alongside. A dinner that costs almost nothing, feeds a family, and freezes perfectly for future meals.

**Lentil Enchiladas** — Seasoned cooked lentils mixed with black beans and cheese, rolled into corn tortillas, covered with enchilada sauce and a little cheese, and baked until bubbly. A meatless enchilada that's filling and satisfying and works for people who don't typically eat meatless enchiladas.

**Homemade Veggie Burgers** — Cooked lentils mashed with oats, egg, garlic, onion, and spices, formed into patties and pan-fried or baked until crispy on the outside. A homemade veggie burger that actually holds together and has enough protein and fiber to satisfy as a complete meal.

**Lentil Lasagna** — Cooked lentils folded into the tomato meat sauce layer of a lasagna, stretching the meat further and adding protein and fiber. Most people eating it won't notice anything different — they'll just notice the lasagna is particularly good.

## Side Dishes

Lentils make some of the most satisfying side dishes in the kitchen — preparations that stand on their own as a complement to any protein.

**Seasoned Lentils** — Brown or green lentils cooked with garlic, onion, cumin, and a splash of olive oil. A simple, versatile side dish that works alongside roasted chicken, grilled fish, or any grain-based bowl. Season generously — lentils absorb salt and need more than you think.

**Lemon Herb Lentils** — Cooked lentils tossed warm with fresh lemon juice, olive oil, garlic, fresh parsley, and mint. A bright, fresh side dish that works particularly well alongside Mediterranean proteins like grilled chicken or lamb.

**Mediterranean Lentils** — Cooked lentils with roasted tomatoes, kalamata olives, fresh herbs, olive oil, and a little red wine vinegar. A side dish that works warm or at room temperature and pairs with almost any protein.

**Lentil Rice Pilaf** — Brown lentils and long-grain brown rice cooked together with caramelized onion, cumin, and broth — a dish known across the Middle East as mujaddara. The caramelized onion is the defining element and should not be rushed. Top with a dollop of Greek yogurt and fresh herbs. One of the most satisfying side dishes there is.

**Curried Lentils** — Red lentils cooked with garlic, ginger, curry powder, coconut milk, and a handful of spinach until thick and creamy. A side dish that becomes the most interesting thing on the plate and pairs particularly well with roasted chicken or grilled salmon.

**Warm Lentil Salad** — Green or black lentils tossed warm with roasted vegetables, Dijon vinaigrette, and fresh herbs. A side dish that works as a standalone lunch or as a warm component in a dinner spread.

## Snacks and Appetizers

**Lentil Hummus** — Cooked red lentils blended with tahini, lemon, garlic, and olive oil. A protein-rich dip that works with the same dippers as traditional chickpea hummus — vegetables, whole grain crackers, or pita. The lentil version is slightly lighter in texture and has a more earthy flavor.

**Crispy Roasted Lentils** — Cooked lentils patted dry, tossed with olive oil and seasoning, and roasted at 400°F for 20 to 25 minutes until crispy and slightly crunchy. A high-protein snack that travels well and works as a topping for salads and soups.

**Lentil Dip** — Cooked brown lentils blended with roasted garlic, olive oil, lemon, and cumin into a thick, spreadable dip. Serve with whole grain crackers or sliced vegetables. A dip with more protein and fiber than most options on a snack table.

**Lentil Patties** — Cooked lentils mashed with oats, egg, garlic, and spices, formed into small rounds and pan-fried until crispy. Serve with Greek yogurt dip and fresh herbs. An appetizer that works as a light dinner alongside a salad.

**Stuffed Mushrooms with Lentils** — Large mushroom caps filled with a mixture of cooked seasoned lentils, garlic, herbs, and a little parmesan, then baked at 375°F for 20 minutes. An elegant appetizer that's plant-forward without announcing itself as such.

**Lettuce Wraps** — Seasoned lentils with diced cucumber, shredded carrot, green onion, and a drizzle of sesame-ginger dressing, spooned into butter lettuce leaves. A light, fresh appetizer that works as a starter or a snack.

## Lentils as a Recipe Transformation Tool

Lentils are one of the most effective recipe transformation ingredients because they extend meat-based dishes, add protein and fiber to vegetable-based dishes, and integrate into comfort food without announcing their presence. Old2New can help you use them to upgrade the recipes your family already loves:

- **Spaghetti sauce** — replace a third to half the ground beef with cooked brown lentils; the texture and heartiness remain, the saturated fat drops significantly
- **Chili** — add a cup of cooked lentils alongside the beans for extra protein and body
- **Tacos** — use half ground turkey and half cooked lentils for the filling
- **Meatloaf** — blend cooked lentils into the ground meat mixture for moisture and extended portions
- **Meatballs** — same principle — lentils add moisture and extend the meat
- **Soups and stews** — add a handful of red lentils to any soup and they'll dissolve and thicken the broth naturally
- **Casseroles** — fold cooked lentils into any casserole to add protein and fiber without changing the flavor

The comfort stays. The recipe just works a little better.

## How to Choose and Store Lentils

Brown lentils are the most versatile and the best starting point — they work in soups, chili, casseroles, and everyday cooking and hold their shape moderately well. Green lentils are firmer and hold their shape better than brown, making them ideal for salads and grain bowls where texture matters. Red lentils cook faster than other varieties and dissolve into soups, curries, and sauces — they're the best choice for creamy applications where a smooth, thick texture is the goal. Black lentils (sometimes called beluga lentils) are small, firm, and visually striking — excellent for salads and elegant side dishes.

Store dry lentils in an airtight container in a cool, dry pantry. They keep for over a year. Cooked lentils refrigerate in an airtight container for up to five days and freeze perfectly — batch-cook a large amount and freeze in one-cup portions for weeknight convenience.

Unlike dried beans, lentils require no soaking. Rinse, add to a pot with cold water in a 1:2 ratio, bring to a simmer, and cook until tender — about 20 minutes for red lentils, 25 to 30 minutes for brown and green, and 25 minutes for black. Season after cooking rather than before, as salt added during cooking can toughen the lentils slightly.

## Don't Let Lentils Go to Waste

Lentils don't really go to waste — dry lentils keep for over a year, and cooked lentils that have been in the refrigerator for a few days are still perfect for cooking. Leftover cooked lentils can go into any soup, stir fry, grain bowl, taco filling, or pasta sauce. They can be mashed into a dip or blended into a sauce. They can be tossed with whatever vegetables are in the refrigerator and eaten warm or cold. They're one of the most versatile leftovers in the kitchen — genuinely difficult to waste if you cook with them regularly.

## FAQ

**Do lentils need to be soaked before cooking?**
No — unlike dried beans, lentils cook without soaking. Rinse them well, add to a pot with cold water, and simmer until tender. Red lentils take about 20 minutes. Brown and green lentils take 25 to 30 minutes. Black lentils take about 25 minutes.

**Why do my lentils turn mushy?**
Red lentils are designed to dissolve — that's their purpose in soups and curries. For lentils that should hold their shape (green, black), use a 1:2 water ratio, bring to a gentle simmer rather than a boil, and check them early. Overcooked lentils can't be undone, so start checking at 20 minutes.

**Can I substitute lentils for meat in recipes?**
Yes, particularly in applications where ground meat is used — tacos, Bolognese, chili, meatloaf, and stuffed peppers all work well with lentils replacing part or all of the meat. The texture is different but the protein content and satiety are comparable. Starting with a half-and-half replacement is usually the easiest transition.

**Are lentils good for people on GLP-1 medications?**
Lentils are exceptionally well-suited for anyone managing appetite or portions. The combination of protein and fiber is among the highest of any plant-based food, which means meals built on lentils are genuinely filling relative to their caloric content. The fiber also slows digestion and supports steady blood sugar levels.

**What's the best lentil for beginners?**
Brown lentils — they're the most forgiving, the most widely available, the least expensive, and the most versatile. Start with a simple lentil soup and go from there.
